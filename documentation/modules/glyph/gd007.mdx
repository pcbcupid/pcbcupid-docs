The GD007-Glyph RP2040 is a breakout board built around the Raspberry Pi RP2040 microcontroller, integrating a Li-Po battery charger (MCP73831) and a low-dropout regulator for portable and embedded applications. It exposes 26 GPIO pins, SWD debug interfaces, and power management pins through through-hole pads on a compact 50.8 × 22.9 mm board. The board is designed for breadboard prototyping, wearable electronics, and battery-powered projects where a full development board is overkill.

## Pin Configuration

- **RESET (Pin 1)** — Active-low reset input; pull low to reset the RP2040.
- **+3.3V (Pins 2, 3)** — Regulated 3.3 V output from the onboard LDO; can also be used as an input to power the board.
- **GND (Pins 4, 16, 18)** — Common ground connections.
- **GPIO_00 (Pin 5)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as UART0 TX.
- **GPIO_01 (Pin 6)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as UART0 RX.
- **GPIO_02 (Pin 7)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI0 SCK.
- **GPIO_03 (Pin 8)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI0 TX.
- **GPIO_08 (Pin 9)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI0 RX.
- **GPIO_05 (Pin 10)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as I2C0 SDA.
- **GPIO_06 (Pin 11)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as I2C0 SCL.
- **GPIO_07 (Pin 12)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as PWM output.
- **GPIO_04 (Pin 13)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI0 CS.
- **GPIO_13 (Pin 14)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as PWM output.
- **GPIO_12 (Pin 15)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as PWM output.
- **SWCLK (Pin 17)** — Serial wire debug clock for programming and debugging.
- **SWDIO (Pin 19)** — Serial wire debug data I/O for programming and debugging.
- **+BATT (Pin 20)** — Direct connection to the Li-Po battery positive terminal; also the charger output.
- **L_EN (Pin 21)** — LDO enable pin; drive high to enable the 3.3 V regulator, low to disable.
- **VBUS (Pin 22)** — USB 5 V input; also used as the charger input.
- **GPIO_23 (Pin 23)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI1 SCK.
- **GPIO_24 (Pin 24)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI1 TX.
- **GPIO_25 (Pin 25)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI1 RX.
- **GPIO_26 (Pin 26)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as ADC0 (12-bit ADC input).
- **GPIO_27 (Pin 27)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as ADC1.
- **GPIO_28 (Pin 28)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as ADC2.
- **GPIO_29 (Pin 29)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as ADC3.
- **GPIO_20 (Pin 30)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as SPI1 CS.
- **GPIO_21 (Pin 31)** — General-purpose I/O; also usable as I2C0 SDA (alternate function).

## Key Features

- **Raspberry Pi RP2040** dual-core Arm Cortex-M0+ processor running at up to 133 MHz.
- **26 exposed GPIO pins** with support for SPI, I2C, UART, PWM, and 4-channel 12-bit ADC.
- **MCP73831 Li-Po charger** with 4.2 V charge voltage and programmable charge current via external resistor.
- **Onboard LDO regulator** providing a stable 3.3 V rail from battery or USB input.
- **SWD debug interface** (SWCLK/SWDIO) for programming and debugging with standard tools like Picoprobe or a Raspberry Pi Debug Probe.
- **VBUS and +BATT pins** for flexible power input — USB 5 V or single-cell Li-Po battery.
- **L_EN enable pin** for controlling the 3.3 V regulator output, useful for low-power sleep modes.
- **Compact 50.8 × 22.9 mm form factor** with 1.7 mm through-hole pads on 2.54 mm pitch, breadboard-compatible.
- **Reset pin** for external reset control.

## Applications

- **Battery-powered wearables** — the integrated Li-Po charger and LDO make it ideal for compact, rechargeable devices.
- **Breadboard prototyping** — through-hole pads on standard 2.54 mm pitch allow direct insertion into solderless breadboards.
- **Portable sensor nodes** — 4 ADC channels and multiple I2C/SPI interfaces support environmental sensing and data logging.
- **Custom keyboard or macro pads** — 26 GPIOs provide ample matrix scanning and LED driving capability.
- **Embedded control systems** — dual-core processing and rich peripheral set suit motor control, display driving, and automation tasks.
- **Educational projects** — SWD debugging and standard RP2040 toolchain support make it a solid learning platform.

## Precaution Note

- **Input voltage limits** — VBUS must be kept within 4.5–5.5 V; +BATT is intended for a single-cell Li-Po (3.0–4.2 V). Exceeding these ranges can damage the charger or LDO.
- **Battery polarity** — Connecting a battery with reversed polarity to +BATT/GND will permanently damage the MCP73831 charger and potentially the RP2040.
- **Current draw** — The onboard LDO is not designed for high-current loads; keep total 3.3 V draw below approximately 300 mA to avoid thermal shutdown or voltage droop.
- **GPIO voltage levels** — All GPIO pins are 3.3 V logic only; applying 5 V to any GPIO pin can damage the RP2040.
- **L_EN behavior** — When L_EN is pulled low, the 3.3 V rail is disabled; ensure all connected peripherals are powered down or isolated to prevent back-feeding the regulator.
- **ADC input range** — ADC pins (GPIO_26–29) accept 0–3.3 V only; higher voltages require an external voltage divider.
- **Charger thermal considerations** — The MCP73831 may heat up during fast charging; ensure adequate airflow or reduce charge current via the PROG resistor for sustained operation.
