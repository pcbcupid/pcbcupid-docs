---
title: "Glyph RP2040 Overview"
description: "A 50.8×22.9 mm RP2040 module with 23 GPIOs, USB-C, and battery charging in a compact form factor."
sidebarTitle: "Overview"
---

The Glyph RP2040 is a compact, castellated module built around the Raspberry Pi RP2040 dual-core microcontroller. Designed for embedded projects that need a balance of GPIO flexibility, USB connectivity, and battery-powered operation, the Glyph RP2040 integrates a Li-Po charge controller and a regulated 3.3 V supply directly on the board. Its 50.8 × 22.9 mm footprint with through-hole pads makes it suitable for both breadboard prototyping and permanent soldered assemblies.

- **Dimensions**: 50.8 × 22.9 mm (2.0 × 0.9 in)
- **Processor**: Raspberry Pi RP2040, dual-core Arm Cortex-M0+ at up to 133 MHz
- **RAM**: 264 KB SRAM
- **Flash**: 2 MB QSPI flash (on-module)
- **GPIO**: 23 exposed GPIO pins (GPIO00–GPIO08, GPIO12, GPIO13, GPIO20, GPIO21, GPIO23–GPIO29), all with digital I/O; select pins support ADC, PWM, I2C, SPI, and UART functions
- **Power**: USB-C (VBUS) or battery input (+BATT), with onboard MCP73831 Li-Po charger and 3.3 V regulator

### Connectivity

The Glyph RP2040 exposes the RP2040's full peripheral set across its GPIO pins. Two dedicated power pins (+3.3 V and GND) are provided on both sides of the module for convenient power distribution. The USB-C connector handles both programming and power delivery, while the +BATT pin allows direct connection of a single-cell Li-Po battery for portable applications.

### Development Support

The RP2040 is natively supported by the Raspberry Pi Pico SDK, MicroPython, CircuitPython, and the Arduino IDE. The Glyph RP2040 presents as a standard RP2040 device over USB, so no special drivers or board definitions are required — it works with the standard Pico tooling. The RESET pin is broken out for external reset control.

### Memory and Storage

The module carries 2 MB of QSPI flash, providing ample space for firmware images, filesystem storage, or data logging. The RP2040's 264 KB SRAM is fully available for application use.

### Battery Charging

An MCP73831 charge management controller is integrated on the module, providing constant-current/constant-voltage charging for single-cell Li-Po/Li-Ion batteries at 4.2 V. The charge current is set by an onboard programming resistor; the STAT output indicates charge status. This allows the Glyph RP2040 to be used in battery-powered designs without requiring an external charger circuit.

### Programming and Bootloader

The RP2040 uses a built-in USB bootloader: hold the BOOT button (if present on your carrier board) while connecting USB to enter mass-storage mode, then drag-and-drop a UF2 firmware file. Alternatively, the SWD interface can be used with a debug probe for advanced development.

> **Note**: The Glyph RP2040 is a module intended for integration into a larger PCB or breadboard circuit. It does not include a USB connector or BOOT button on the module itself — these must be provided by the carrier board. The USB-C connector is not part of the module footprint.

The Glyph RP2040 exposes 28 through-hole pins arranged in two rows of 14. Pin 1 is marked with a square pad on the top row (left side when viewed from the top with the text upright). All GPIO pins are 3.3 V logic level and are not 5 V tolerant.

### Power Pins

| Pin | Name | Description |
|-----|------|-------------|
| 2, 3 | +3.3 V | Regulated 3.3 V output (from onboard regulator) |
| 4, 16 | GND | Ground reference |
| 17 | +BATT | Battery positive input (3.0–4.2 V Li-Po) |
| 19 | VBUS | USB 5 V input (from USB-C connector on carrier) |

| Pin | Name | Alternate Functions |
|-----|------|-------------------|
| 5 | GPIO00 | I2C0 SDA, SPI0 RX, UART0 TX |

| 7 | GPIO02 | SPI0 SCK, UART0 CTS |
| 8 | GPIO03 | SPI0 TX, UART0 RTS |
| 9 | GPIO08 | I2C0 SDA (alt), SPI1 RX, UART1 TX |
| 10 | GPIO05 | SPI0 CS (alt), UART1 RX |
| 11 | GPIO06 | SPI0 SCK (alt), UART1 CTS |

| 20 | GPIO23 | I2C0 SCL (alt), SPI1 CS, UART0 CTS |

| Pin | Name | Description |
|-----|------|-------------|
| 1 | RESET | Active-low reset input (pull to GND to reset) |
| 18 | L_EN | Regulator enable (high = enabled, pull high for normal operation) |

### ADC Pins

The RP2040 ADC is available on GPIO25–GPIO29 (pins 22–26). The ADC reference is 3.3 V, providing a resolution of approximately 3.22 mV per LSB (12-bit). Note that GPIO25 also serves as ADC2 but is shared with the onboard temperature sensor — reading ADC2 with the temperature sensor disabled gives the external pin value.

> **Note**: The Glyph RP2040 does not include a Qwiic/Stemma QT connector on the module itself. I2C is available on multiple pin pairs (e.g., GPIO00/GPIO01 or GPIO20/GPIO21) for connecting external Qwiic devices via a carrier board.

---
title: "Glyph RP2040 Assembly"
description: "Step-by-step guide for soldering headers or wires to the Glyph RP2040 module."
sidebarTitle: "Assembly"
---

The Glyph RP2040 is designed for through-hole soldering. The module has 28 plated through-hole pads (1.7 mm diameter, 1.0 mm drill) arranged in two rows on 2.54 mm (0.1 in) pitch. You can solder male headers, female headers, or wires directly to these pads.

### Header Installation

1. **Choose your headers**: Standard 2.54 mm breakaway headers work well. For breadboard use, solder male headers with the pins pointing down (away from the module). For mounting the module on another PCB, use female headers or solder the module directly.

2. **Position the headers**: Insert the long ends of male header pins through the module's through-holes from the top side. The module should sit flat on the header's plastic spacer. For female headers, place them on the bottom side of the module.

3. **Secure the assembly**: Place the module with headers on a flat surface or in a vise. If using a breadboard, you can insert the header pins into the breadboard to hold everything aligned during soldering.

4. **Solder the pins**: Apply solder to each pad, ensuring the solder flows through the hole and forms a fillet on both sides. Use a soldering iron set to 350–370 °C (660–700 °F) with 0.5–0.8 mm solder. Complete all 28 pins.

5. **Inspect**: Check for cold joints, solder bridges between adjacent pins, or insufficient solder. The pad pitch is 2.54 mm, so bridges are unlikely but possible with excessive solder.

### Direct Wire Soldering

For permanent installations, you can solder wires directly to the through-holes:

1. Strip 2–3 mm of insulation from each wire.
2. Tin the exposed wire ends.
3. Insert each wire through its pad from the bottom side.
4. Solder from the top side, filling the hole completely.
5. Add strain relief by securing wires to the module with a dab of hot glue or epoxy.

### Reflow Soldering

The Glyph RP2040 can also be reflow-soldered onto a carrier PCB using solder paste and a stencil. The module's flat bottom surface and through-hole pads make it suitable for paste-in-hole reflow. Apply solder paste to the carrier board's pads, place the module, and reflow using a standard lead-free profile (peak 245 °C).

> **Note**: The module's through-holes are 1.0 mm diameter — standard 0.1 in headers fit snugly. If you encounter resistance inserting headers, check for solder obstructions in the holes.

---
title: "Glyph RP2040 Power Management"
description: "Power options for the Glyph RP2040: USB, battery, and external supplies, plus battery voltage measurement."
sidebarTitle: "Power Management"
---

The Glyph RP2040 is designed for flexible powering: it can run from USB, a single-cell Li-Po battery, or an external regulated supply. The onboard power architecture routes power through an MCP73831 battery charger and a 3.3 V regulator.

### USB Power

Connect a USB-C cable to the carrier board's USB-C connector, which routes 5 V to the VBUS pin (pin 19). The onboard regulator converts this to 3.3 V for the RP2040 and the +3.3 V output pins. USB power is the simplest option for development and bench testing.

### Battery Power

Connect a single-cell Li-Po/Li-Ion battery (3.0–4.2 V) to the +BATT pin (pin 17) and GND. The MCP73831 charger provides constant-current/constant-voltage charging at 4.2 V when USB power is also present. The charge current is set by an onboard resistor (typically 500 mA, depending on the resistor value).

When USB is disconnected, the battery directly powers the 3.3 V regulator. The RP2040 operates down to approximately 3.0 V input, so the battery can be discharged to its safe cutoff voltage.

### Power Path

The module uses a simple power path: VBUS and +BATT are diode-ORed (or similar) to the regulator input. When both USB and battery are connected, the higher voltage source (typically USB at 5 V) powers the system while the charger tops up the battery. When USB is removed, the battery seamlessly takes over.

### Measuring Battery Voltage

To monitor battery level, connect a voltage divider from +BATT to an ADC-capable GPIO (GPIO26–GPIO29). Use two resistors (e.g., 100 kΩ and 100 kΩ) to divide the 4.2 V max battery voltage down to the 3.3 V ADC reference range. The RP2040's ADC is 12-bit, giving approximately 3.22 mV resolution at the pin.

### Alternative Power Options

- **External 3.3 V**: You can bypass the onboard regulator by supplying 3.3 V directly to the +3.3 V pins (pins 2 and 3). In this case, do not connect VBUS or +BATT, and ensure the L_EN pin (pin 18) is pulled high or left floating.
- **External 5 V**: Supply 5 V to the VBUS pin from a bench supply or other source. The onboard regulator handles the conversion to 3.3 V.
- **Regulator disable**: Pull L_EN low to disable the 3.3 V regulator, useful in low-power sleep applications where external power management is preferred.

### Power Consumption

The RP2040's power consumption depends heavily on clock speed and workload:

- **Active (133 MHz)**: ~30–50 mA at 3.3 V
- **Sleep**: ~1–2 mA with peripherals disabled
- **Dormant**: ~180 µA (RTC running)

For battery-powered designs, consider using the RP2040's sleep modes and disabling unused peripherals to extend battery life.

> **Warning**: The +BATT pin accepts only 3.0–4.2 V. Do not connect higher voltages (e.g., 5 V) to +BATT, as this will damage the charger and regulator. Always observe correct battery polarity — reverse connection will destroy the module. The MCP73831 charger is designed for single-cell Li-Po/Li-Ion batteries only; do not use with other battery chemistries.
